Overpass plates, Atkinson Hyperlegible body, Readex Pro Arabic. Bands encode footprint: green low, blue water, amber heat and crowd.

Why Sharjah

The three route bands take their colors from places on the routes themselves: Blue Souk tile blue, Kalba mangrove green, and Mleiha's amber rock. The neutral board recalls the whitewashed courtyards of the Sharjah Art Foundation and the coral-stone of the Heart of Sharjah. Bilingual plates follow the road and museum signs visitors already read along the Corniche.

Why sustainable

Every route band is a footprint label: green for walking and shaded paths, blue for creek water transport, amber for motorized or peak-heat legs. Crowd gauges borrow the parking-sign idiom to disperse visitors toward Mleiha, Kalba, and Khor Fakkan. Flat print-grade graphics run on low-end phones without heavy rendering.

Risks

Judges may read the sternness as a transport utility rather than a tourism product, so the photography has to carry the warmth in the deck. Eight bespoke pictograms is the ceiling in seven hours; the fallback is Material Symbols with credit in the disclosure document. Overpass has tight apertures below 14px, so small labels must use Atkinson. Readex Pro is a legibility face, not a calligraphic one, and Arabic-first judges may find it plain. Two contrast rules must live in the CSS tokens: amber never carries white text (3.22:1) and green text never sits on the dark plate (2.53:1). Three band colors can collide with basemap colors, so the map base must stay desaturated gray.
