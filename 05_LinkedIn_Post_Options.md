## Option B: what we built (about 260 words)

On Saturday morning, Team Amadeus walked into the American University of Sharjah with an idea for a tourist guide. On Sunday evening we presented Daleel on stage as one of ten finalist teams at the Sharjah Tourism Innovation Hackathon 2026. We did not take a prize. The product still runs, and this post is about what four people managed to build in that time.

Daleel (دليل, "guide") opens in a phone's browser, so a visitor scans a QR code and starts. It plans the day around heat, crowds, and travel time, and suggests a later hour or a quieter place when the first choice would be crowded or too hot. Five Sharjah places are built in 3D, including a walk-through of the Central Souq with 104 shops on two levels and an AR guide that leads you to the shop you pick. At Al Hisn Fort, you can switch between the fort in 1823, in 1969, and today. The guide answers in English and Arabic. Recycling a bottle near a site earns points for vouchers at local shops, and the app can be controlled by voice, head movement, or a wink, which Rama and Khadeja brought over from their Sahm research project.

By pitch time, Daleel had also become a business: touchscreen navigator tables for malls, parks, and heritage sites, paid for by the venues and free for visitors.

Rama Saud Abdallah designed it, Khadeja Alkhazraji tested and shipped every build, Sulaymon Sadullo ran our visitor survey and data, and I built the product with Claude Code and wrote the pitch. Thank you to AUS, the Sharjah Commerce and Tourism Development Authority, Sheraa, and the mentors.

## Option C: the survey as the opening (about 200 words)

Half of the 50 people we surveyed on the first day of the Sharjah Tourism Innovation Hackathon had never heard of Mleiha. More than half had never heard of Al Noor Island, and 40% had visited none of the seven Sharjah attractions we listed.

The sample was small and mostly students living in Abu Dhabi, so we treated it as a signal rather than a market study. It still shaped what we built. People cannot visit places they do not know exist, and Sharjah's official guide app was last updated in December 2022.

Daleel (دليل, "guide") is our response: a free web app, plus touchscreen navigator tables in malls, parks, and heritage sites that draw the route in 3D and pass it to your phone with a QR code. The plan accounts for heat, crowds, and travel time, and the guide answers in English and Arabic.

Team Amadeus from Abu Dhabi University made the final ten out of 800 registered participants and pitched on stage at the American University of Sharjah. We did not place.

Thank you to Rama Saud Abdallah, Khadeja Alkhazraji, and Sulaymon Sadullo, and to AUS, the Sharjah Commerce and Tourism Development Authority, Sheraa, and the mentors.

## Option D: what the weekend taught us (about 250 words, one sentence left for you)

The hardest part of the Sharjah Tourism Innovation Hackathon came before we wrote any code. On the first afternoon we learned that last year's winning team had built an AR tourism app with stamps, badges, and curated routes, and the organizers had used an AI tourism assistant as their worked example on stage. Our first plan was close to both, so we had to find the part that was ours.

We kept the AR and the AI guide as tools and built Daleel around a different question: when should a visitor go, and how should they get there? By Sunday the app planned each day around heat, crowds, and travel time, and the pitch had become a navigator table for malls, parks, and heritage sites that carries the route to the visitor's phone.

We also used AI throughout, mainly Claude Code, Gemini, and Codex, and declared every tool and every generated component in the disclosure document the organizers required. Their guidelines say the judges assess the team's originality, understanding, and technical contribution rather than the tool's output, so we treated the disclosure as part of the work.
